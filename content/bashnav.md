bashnav is six tools for a boat with no signal. Each one installs as its own
icon on the iPad and runs with the antenna down: no account, no subscription,
and nothing sent anywhere. What you work out stays on your own device.

**Open it:** [bashnav.navigationtools.org](https://bashnav.navigationtools.org/)

## celnav — celestial navigation

{{< figure src="/images/bashnav/celnav-fix.png" alt="Three star sights reduced, with the fix and the plot" class="app" >}}

1. **Sight reduction** for the sun, moon, planets and 57 navigational stars,
   with the whole correction chain: index error, dip, refraction, parallax,
   semi-diameter.
2. **The fix from several sights,** plotted, with the error ellipse — so you
   can see how good it is, not just where it says you are.
3. **A running fix** advances each earlier sight for the run between it and
   the last.
4. **Its own almanac,** worked out on the device. No tables to buy and nothing
   to download each year.
5. **Thirty-three lessons** that teach the method rather than the buttons,
   from why a sight works to the shape of the triangle you are solving.
6. **The camera** is a third way of getting an altitude in, not the first one.

## colregs — the rules of the road

{{< figure src="/images/bashnav/colregs-lights.svg" alt="A vessel's lights drawn as you would see them from a bearing" class="app" >}}

1. **Twenty-eight vessel types** drawn as you would actually see them: the
   lights at the bearing you are on, not a diagram from the side.
2. **Shapes and sound signals,** with what each one means in the Convention.
3. **Encounters:** who gives way, who stands on, and the rule that says so.
4. **Contacts** — bearing drift, closest point of approach, and a tracking
   exercise built on manual methods rather than a plotter.
5. **Night mode** is red on black, because dark adaptation takes twenty
   minutes to build and a second to lose.

## tides — harmonic prediction

1. **8,334 stations worldwide,** searched by name or by where you are.
2. **Heights and times** for the day, worked out from the harmonic
   constituents on the device.
3. **No network, ever.** The constituents ship with the tool.

## weather — your own barometer

1. **Read your own glass:** the pressure, how fast it is moving, and what that
   has meant historically.
2. **Write your forecast down before the tool shows you its own,** and it
   scores you against what actually happened.
3. **The physics underneath** — why the wind blows along the isobars and not
   across them, and what a veering wind tells you.

## deck-log — the boat's records

1. **The deck log, the engine, provisions and spares,** in UTC, append-only.
2. **Nothing is ever deleted.** Two entries that disagree are both kept and
   flagged, which is what a paper log does when you line through and annotate.
3. **Export and merge:** carry the log off the boat, bring another copy back,
   and the two merge without losing a record.

## drill — your own study material

drill ships **empty**. It holds no questions and no material of its own: you
load a pack from your own device, it is read in the browser, and it never
leaves the device it is on.

## Putting them on the iPad

1. Open [bashnav.navigationtools.org](https://bashnav.navigationtools.org/) in
   Safari, with a signal.
2. Open the tool you want, then tap **Share → Add to Home Screen**.
3. Open it once **from its Home Screen icon**, still with a signal. An
   installed web app keeps its own storage, separate from Safari's, so this
   first opening is what fills its offline copy.
4. Repeat for each tool you will use at sea.

Then prove it at the dock rather than finding out at sea: open every screen,
turn the network off, and relaunch from the icon.

## What they are, and are not

Training aids and working tools, not a substitute for judgement. Where any of
these and the published rules, almanac or chart differ, the published source
is right and this is wrong.
